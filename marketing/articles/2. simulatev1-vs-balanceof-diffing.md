# Twitter Thread: eth_simulateV1 vs balanceOf Diffing

---

## Tweet 1

After our last thread, a lot of people asked: "Why not just use eth_simulateV1?"

Fair question. Here's the tradeoff between parsing logs (simulateV1) vs diffing balances (what we do) — and why neither is strictly better.

> **[IMAGE: Comparison table — see "Tweet 1 Image" in Image Generation Prompts below]**

---

## Tweet 2

Quick primer: eth_simulateV1 is a superset of eth_call that also returns event logs. With `traceTransfers: true`, it even emits synthetic Transfer events for native ETH moves.

One RPC call → full logs → parse Transfer events → done.

Spec authored by @AKillariDev, shipped in all 4 major clients (Geth 1.14.9+, Nethermind, Reth, Besu).

---

## Tweet 3

Our approach: inject a ghost contract at the user's address via eth_call state overrides. It snapshots `balanceOf()` before and after executing the tx, returns the diff.

No logs parsed. We measure what actually changed in the wallet.

(Full breakdown in our previous thread)

---

## Tweet 4

The core difference:

**simulateV1**: "What events did the EVM emit?"
**balanceOf diffing**: "What's the balance before vs after?"

Usually the same answer. But not always — and the edge cases matter.

---

## Tweet 5

**Rebasing tokens** (stETH, aTokens, sOHM) change your balance without emitting Transfer events. stETH uses a shares model — when the pool grows, your `balanceOf` increases silently. Emitting a Transfer per holder per rebase would be an unbounded loop.

Log parsing: misses this.
Balance diffing: catches it.

---

## Tweet 6

**Fee-on-transfer tokens** — the Transfer event amount is implementation-dependent. SafeMoon emits the net amount (after fee). Other tokens may not. There's no standard.

Balance diffing doesn't care. It just measures what the user actually received.

---

## Tweet 7

**Where simulateV1 wins:**

- 1 RPC call vs our 2 (createAccessList + eth_call)
- Multi-tx simulation is native — state persists between calls
- No bytecode injection = no Permit2 extcodesize workaround needed
- Simpler code — parse a logs array vs maintaining a ghost contract

These are real advantages.

---

## Tweet 8

**Where balanceOf diffing wins:**

- Catches rebasing tokens and non-compliant tokens that skip events
- Measures actual net balance change, not event data
- Works on any Berlin+ chain (Apr 2021) — simulateV1 needs Geth 1.14.9+ (Sep 2024)
- eth_call is the most universally supported RPC method

---

## Tweet 9

**What about debug_traceCall?**

Not viable for a wallet:
- 40x more expensive on RPC providers (1000 CU vs 26 on Alchemy)
- Rarely available on free/public endpoints
- Doesn't return logs — raw call trees only
- 10-100x slower
- trace_call isn't even supported by Geth

Great for debugging. Not for real-time UX.

---

## Tweet 10

| | simulateV1 | balanceOf diffing |
|---|---|---|
| Standard ERC-20 | Correct | Correct |
| Native ETH | Correct (traceTransfers) | Correct |
| Fee-on-transfer | Implementation-dependent | Always correct |
| Rebasing tokens | Missed | Caught |
| RPC calls | 1 | 2 |
| Multi-tx | Native | Custom contract |
| Chain support | Geth 1.14.9+ | Any Berlin+ chain |

---

## Tweet 11

The two approaches aren't competing — they're complementary.

We're considering: try simulateV1 first, fall back to balanceOf diffing on unsupported chains, and use balance checks as a supplementary layer for rebasing tokens regardless.

Full simulation code is open source. ~190 lines of Solidity, ~1,200 lines of TypeScript. No API keys.
